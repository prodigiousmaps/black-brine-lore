# Cult of the Forgotten Wind

## Overview
After centuries of isolation, jungle survival, and devotion to a false god, the
Cult of the Forgotten Wind has degenerated into a monstrous hierarchy of
fanatics. What was once a coherent culture has collapsed into a caste-bound
theocracy sustained by ritual violence, superstition, and the promise of divine
return.

The cult believes their goddess—an imprisoned wind djinn—is destined to be
freed by a prophesied ship and its crew. They await this moment with absolute
certainty and lethal devotion.

## Beliefs
- The wind is sacred and omnipresent.
- Flesh is temporary; transformation is inevitable.
- Suffering prepares the faithful for ascension.
- The goddess’s return will remake the world—and her followers.

## Structure
The cult is divided into three rigid castes, each serving a specific function in
their survival and worship.

### The Howling Ones (Lowest Caste – Savage Hunters)
Once the common folk of the cult, the Howling Ones have lost nearly all trace of
humanity.

- Physically warped by generations of jungle life and ritual mutilation.
- Travel through the canopy using elongated limbs and hooked claws.
- Do not speak; communicate through guttural howls.
- Bodies carved with binding sigils that enforce obedience.

**Tactics**
- Canopy ambush and silent drops.
- Frenzied attacks triggered by blood.
- Preternatural reflexes that make escape difficult.

**Signs**
- Blood-drained corpses hung high in trees.
- Scratched warning sigils in bark and roots.
- Distant, overlapping howls with no clear source.

---

### The Stormborn (Middle Caste – Zealous Warriors)
The Stormborn retain intelligence, language, and tactical thinking.

- Serve as warriors, hunters, and ritual leaders.
- Command the Howling Ones in coordinated attacks.
- Covered in ritual scars believed to strengthen their bond to the wind.
- Speak a broken, hissing dialect of their ancient tongue.

**Tactics**
- Coordinated pack assaults.
- Primal air magic used to disorient enemies.
- Hardened skin resistant to minor harm.

**Signs**
- Bone-and-cloth effigies marking jungle crossings.
- Deep rhythmic chanting echoing through foliage.
- Fresh sacrifices prepared for ritual exposure.

---

### The Windcallers (Highest Caste – Eldritch Priesthood)
The Windcallers are the last fully self-aware members of the cult and its true
leaders.

- Physically frail, grotesquely elongated by ritual magic.
- Masters of ancient air sorcery and blood rites.
- Maintain absolute authority over lower castes.
- Fully understand the prophecies and the ship’s significance.

They believe the arrival of the ship heralds their goddess’s awakening and may
view its crew as chosen instruments.

**Tactics**
- Absolute control over cult forces via ritual command.
- Long-distance communication through whispered winds.
- Ability to summon violent storms and localized devastation.

**Signs**
- Unnatural silence in the jungle.
- Stone carvings that repel water and decay.
- Encroaching fog filled with half-seen figures.

## Goals
- Free the imprisoned wind djinn.
- Complete the final rites tied to the prophesied ship.
- Transform themselves into divine servants upon her return.

## Truth of the Cult
The djinn does not love her followers. She despises all mortals, including those
who worship her. If freed, she will consume the cult first—twisting them into
formless manifestations of wind and fury as the opening sacrifice of her return.

## Role in the World
To Black Brine, the cult is a half-forgotten jungle myth.
To scholars, they are a dangerous repository of ancient knowledge.
To the jungle, they are an infection sustained by belief.

## Notable Figures
- **The Windcallers (Collective):** The ruling priesthood; rarely seen, never
  questioned.
- **Stormborn War-Leaders:** Commanders of raids and sacrifices.

## Rumors
- The cult can hear ships before they crest the horizon.
- The wind carries their whispers into dreams.
- A Windcaller has been seen near the coast, watching the sea.
