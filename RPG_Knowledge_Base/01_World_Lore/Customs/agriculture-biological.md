# Black Brine — Agricultural & Biological Production

## Overview
Black Brine’s agriculture is specialized and survival-focused, built around brine-tolerant crops and controlled aquatic harvesting rather than abundance farming.

## Appearance
Fields are low, waterlogged, and segmented by channels of dark brine. Kelp lines, eel traps, and fungal racks are common sights. Workers wear protective gear against salt exposure and mist events.

## Role in Society
Food production ensures baseline stability, but its greater value lies in supporting preservation industries and producing rare biological materials like brineheart.

## Features
- **Brineheart Cultivation:** The island’s signature crop; used for sustenance, preservation, alchemy, and ritual purposes.
- **Salt-Resilient Crops:** Marsh grains, bitter greens, tubers, and kelp-derived food sources.
- **Managed Fisheries:** Eels, shellfish, and kelp harvested under strict rotational systems.
- **Fungal & Algal Growth:** Supplementary food and alchemical inputs grown in controlled environments.
- **Preservation Integration:** Most output is immediately salted, dried, or processed.

## Notable Figures
- **Warden of Yields (Trade Authority):** Oversees quotas, crop licensing, and fishery rotations.

## Rumors
- Certain brineheart strains respond to ritual handling or blood exposure.
- Some fisheries are closed not due to depletion, but because of “changes” in what is being caught.
- Unauthorized crops occasionally appear in restricted zones, then vanish without explanation.
