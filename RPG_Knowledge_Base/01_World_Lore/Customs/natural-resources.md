# Black Brine — Minerals & Natural Resources

## Overview
Black Brine is poor in conventional ore but rich in environmental and anomalous resources. Extraction focuses on what the island produces through salt, mist, and wreckage rather than deep mining.

## Appearance
Resource zones include blinding white salt flats, dark brine pools, coral-stone outcroppings, and wreckage fields half-consumed by tide and sediment. Mist glass zones are unstable and often restricted.

## Role in Society
Resource control underpins the city’s economy. Access to salt, resin sources, and mist-affected zones is tightly licensed, ensuring centralized oversight of all valuable extraction.

## Features
- **Salt & Brine Deposits:** Large-scale production for preservation, trade, and alchemical use.
- **Mist Glass Formation Zones:** Rare sites where mineral sands and supernatural mist fuse into glass.
- **Low-Grade Iron & Trace Ore:** Limited native deposits, supplemented heavily by salvage.
- **Coral Stone & Ballast Rock:** Primary construction materials.
- **Wreckage Fields:** Regulated salvage zones treated as renewable resource sites.

## Notable Figures
- **Surveyor-General of Yields (Trade Authority):** Oversees mapping, quotas, and access rights to all resource zones.

## Rumors
- Certain mist glass deposits form only during specific celestial or tidal alignments.
- Some brine pools dissolve organic matter far faster than natural processes allow.
- Wreckage fields shift overnight, as if moved by unseen currents.
