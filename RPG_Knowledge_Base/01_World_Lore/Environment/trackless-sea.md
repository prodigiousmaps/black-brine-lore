# Trackless Sea

## Overview
The Trackless Sea is the broad expanse of ocean west of Faerûn, stretching beyond the Sword Coast and the Moonshae Isles into waters that few charts agree upon. It is named not for the absence of routes, but for their unreliability—currents shift, landmarks vanish beneath mist, and magical navigation often fails.

While coastal trade routes are well traveled, the deeper reaches of the Trackless Sea remain dangerous, isolating distant islands and fostering cultures cut off from mainland influence.

## Geography & Conditions
The sea is defined by powerful tides, frequent storms, and large regions of persistent fog. Reefs, submerged ruins, and unmarked shoals are common hazards. Weather patterns can change without warning, sometimes defying seasonal logic.

Certain stretches of the Trackless Sea are rumored to distort distance and time, a legacy of ancient conflicts between sea deities and primordial forces.

## Known Regions & Landmarks
- **Moonshae Isles** – Major island chain and primary eastern reference point  
- **Sea of Swords (eastern boundary)** – Heavily trafficked waters near the Sword Coast  
- **Uncharted Western Expanse** – Vast regions marked only by warnings or blank space  
- **Twelvefold Veil Archipelago** – Remote island chain hidden by mist and reefs  

## Role in the World
The Trackless Sea serves as a buffer between Faerûn and the unknown. It is home to pirate fleets, sea cults, isolated kingdoms, and ancient threats best left undisturbed. Control of any safe harbor within the sea grants immense strategic power.

For many mainlanders, the Trackless Sea represents both opportunity and disappearance.

## Navigation & Travel
Navigation relies heavily on local knowledge, seasonal winds, and trusted landmarks such as the Moonshae Isles. Magical navigation is unreliable in certain regions, especially near ancient sites or areas tied to divine conflict.

Sailors speak of “false calms” and “ghost currents” that can draw ships far off course.

## Lore & Beliefs
- Many believe the sea remembers past violence and reacts to it
- Certain storms are thought to be omens or punishments rather than natural events
- Sea gods and forgotten entities are said to contest dominion beneath the waves
